Business Health Diagnostic

Identify the Root Causes Behind Your Business Challenges

When business performance does not match expectations, the visible symptoms rarely tell the whole story. Delayed collections, shrinking margins, rising costs or inconsistent reporting may point to deeper issues across processes, financial controls or management systems.

BookMyRecords' Business Health Diagnostic takes a closer look at the areas affecting your business. Through structured information review, focused discussions and analysis of relevant business processes, we help examine underlying causes, understand their potential impact and identify practical areas for corrective action.

Move Beyond Symptoms to Understand What Needs to Change

  • Root-Cause Focus: Look beyond visible problems to examine the processes, controls and decisions that may be contributing to them.
  • Evidence-Led Assessment: Base observations on available records, relevant business data and discussions with the people involved.
  • Prioritised Corrective Direction: Understand which issues deserve attention first and what further action or specialist input may be required.
Diagnostic Scope

Areas We Can Examine in a Business Health Diagnostic

The diagnostic scope is tailored to the business challenge. Depending on the requirement, it may cover one or several of the following areas.

Margins · Profitability

Financial Performance & Profitability Diagnostic

Examine the drivers behind financial performance and identify areas that may be affecting margins, costs or profitability.

  • • Analysis of available revenue, expense and profitability trends
  • • Review of product, service, customer or business-segment performance where data permits
  • • Examination of major cost heads and unusual movements
  • • Identification of financial performance drivers and areas for further action
Diagnose Profitability
Cash · Liquidity

Cash Flow & Working Capital Diagnostic

Understand why cash may be constrained even when sales or reported profits appear healthy.

  • • Review of cash-flow patterns and short-term liquidity information
  • • Analysis of receivables, payables and inventory data where applicable
  • • Examination of collection cycles and working-capital practices
  • • Identification of potential cash-conversion bottlenecks and improvement areas
Diagnose Cash Flow
Controls · Books

Accounting, Reconciliation & Internal Control Diagnostic

Examine the reliability of accounting processes and the controls that support accurate records.

  • • Review of selected accounting workflows and supporting documentation
  • • Examination of bank, ledger, customer and supplier reconciliation practices
  • • Identification of process weaknesses, control gaps and recurring exceptions
  • • Recommendations for stronger review, approval and documentation practices
Diagnose Controls
Efficiency · Output

Operational Efficiency Diagnostic

Investigate process delays, resource utilisation and recurring inefficiencies that may affect cost, output or customer delivery.

  • • Mapping and review of selected business processes
  • • Identification of bottlenecks, duplication and avoidable manual work
  • • Review of operational cost and productivity information where available
  • • Prioritised opportunities for process improvement and better monitoring
Diagnose Operations
Sales · Commercial

Sales, Customer & Commercial Performance Diagnostic

Understand the business factors influencing sales quality, customer retention and commercial performance.

  • • Review of available sales trends and customer-level information
  • • Analysis of customer concentration, repeat business and outstanding collections where data permits
  • • Examination of pricing, discounting or sales-conversion practices where relevant
  • • Identification of commercial performance questions and improvement opportunities
Diagnose Commercial Performance
Compliance · Risk

Compliance & Business Risk Diagnostic

Review selected compliance processes and business risks that could create operational, financial or regulatory exposure.

  • • Review of relevant compliance calendars, filings and available records
  • • Identification of apparent process or documentation gaps
  • • Review of selected approval, record-retention and responsibility practices
  • • Escalation list of matters requiring detailed professional or legal verification
Diagnose Compliance Risk
From Symptoms to Findings

From Business Symptoms to Actionable Findings

Define the Problem

Agree on the business concern, its context and the outcomes the management team wants to understand.

Examine the Evidence

Review relevant records, reports, workflows and stakeholder inputs within the agreed scope.

Trace Contributing Factors

Connect the findings to potential underlying causes, dependencies and control weaknesses.

Prioritise the Response

Organise findings by business relevance, urgency and the effort or resources likely to be required.

Set Out the Next Steps

  1. Present findings: Explain key observations and their potential business implications.
  2. Recommend options: Outline practical corrective options within the agreed scope.

Frequently Asked Questions on Business Health Diagnostic

Scope, evidence requirements, recommendations, and how a diagnostic differs from audit or certification work.

A Business Health Diagnostic is a focused and deeper assessment of selected business functions or performance challenges. It examines available evidence and processes to understand what may be causing an issue and what corrective actions may be appropriate.

A checkup provides a broad overview and helps identify areas that may need attention. A diagnostic goes deeper into selected areas, examines contributing factors and develops more specific findings and recommendations.

Depending on the agreed scope, it may examine cash-flow pressure, low or inconsistent margins, rising operating costs, delayed collections, accounting discrepancies, process bottlenecks, weak reporting or selected compliance-process gaps.

Requirements depend on the issue being examined. They may include financial statements, ledgers, bank statements, sales and purchase data, inventory information, ageing reports, process documents, compliance records and discussions with relevant team members.

No diagnostic can guarantee that every cause will be established, particularly where records are incomplete or access is limited. The engagement identifies findings and likely contributing factors supported by the information available, and clearly notes material limitations.

Yes. The intended output is to explain key observations, their potential business implications and practical corrective options within the agreed scope. The depth and format of recommendations are confirmed during scoping.

Implementation support can be discussed separately or included where agreed. The nature of support may involve process improvements, accounting and reporting support, compliance assistance or focused business consulting.

No. It is an advisory diagnostic engagement, not a statutory audit, forensic investigation, legal opinion or certification. If the findings indicate a need for any of these specialised services, the matter should be referred to an appropriately qualified professional under a separate engagement.
