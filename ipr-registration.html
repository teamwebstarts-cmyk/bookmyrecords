The Trademark Lifecycle

From Application Filing to the Official ® Symbol

Stage 1
Search & Filing

We conduct clearance checks, file Form TM-A electronically, and issue your official application receipt. You can begin using the ™ mark immediately.

Stage 2
Examination Review

The Registry examines the mark for conflicts. If an Examination Report with objections is issued, our IP attorneys draft a formal legal rebuttal within 30 days.

Stage 3
Journal Publication

Once accepted, your trademark is published in the official weekly Trademark Journal for a mandatory 4-month public opposition period.

Stage 4
Registration & ®

If no third-party opposition is filed, the Registry issues your 10-year Registration Certificate. You obtain statutory ownership and the prestigious ® symbol.

Frequently Asked Questions on Trademarks & IP

Everything you need to know about trademark registration, clearance search, and brand protection in India.

You can start using the ™ symbol immediately upon submitting your Form TM-A application and receiving your official filing receipt number. The ® registered symbol can legally be used only after the Registrar of Trademarks has completed examination, published your mark in the Trade Marks Journal without sustained opposition, and issued the official Trademark Registration Certificate. Using the ® symbol prior to registration is a punishable offense under Section 107 of the Trade Marks Act 1999.

Typically, standard trademark registration takes between 6 and 12 months. An examination report is usually issued within 30 to 60 days. If the mark is accepted without objection, it is advertised in the Trade Marks Journal for a statutory 4-month opposition period. If no third-party opposition is filed, the registration certificate is generated automatically.

Over 65% of trademark applications in India encounter initial objections under Section 9 (lack of distinctiveness) or Section 11 (conflict with existing similar marks). This is a standard procedural step. Our legal IP team drafts a comprehensive, case-law grounded response citing distinctiveness, prior commercial usage, and differences in goods/services within 30 days of the examination report, followed by representation in oral show-cause hearings if required.
